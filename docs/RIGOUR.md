# Governing rigour protocol — 13 September 2026

This protocol and OPEN_ITEMS §0c govern subsequent runs. P17 closed by PI decision as a negative diagnostic result; its original correction gate did not pass. The standing rules below are stronger than flawless historical compliance can support: some P17 plans were written before their judged runs but committed afterwards. D43 records a dispatch-order error; D15 records an unenforced timeout overrun. Preserve these exceptions as evidence, not precedents.

1. **Register and commit before computing judged data.** Freeze the target, question, metrics, thresholds, seeds, budgets, selection rules and stopping conditions in a commit. Save that commit in the run manifest; an uncommitted plan is not sufficient prospectively. D1, D25, D37, D42–D50 illustrate written advance plans, not proof that every historical plan was committed in time. D43 is the explicit ordering exception.
2. **Never move a threshold to suit observed results.** A mistaken criterion needs an argument independent of and predating the data it will judge, a dated correction and fresh confirmation. Keep failed results under the old criterion. D0 imports the P15 criterion history; D43 corrects an exact floating-point equality test and records the premature dispatch. The PI closure addendum changes the stopping rule, not the outcome of any test.
3. **Retain every outcome.** Save failures, timeouts, adverse arms, all registered seeds and all selected parents. No post-hoc choice of flattering replicates. D32/D34 retain the mixed local-axis replication; D39–D49 retain unsuccessful alternatives. D15 retains the unfinished rejection run.
4. **Withdraw by dated addition, never erasure.** When an evidential basis fails, identify affected interpretations, explain why, retain the original text and link the correction. D17–D19 document the bound-history aliasing correction; the final report distinguishes live-point reconstructions from invalid historical bound geometry.
5. **Necessary is not sufficient.** An inactive marginal, agreement in evidence, or success on a toy is each insufficient for posterior certification. D26 recovers a nuisance marginal while retaining wrong active weights; D38 fails the real Beta coverage control; D40 succeeds with unavailable oracle geometry. Require independently consistent active distributions, region occupancy and feasibility-aware predictions too.
6. **Reserve confirmation seeds.** Fix them before development; never use them to tune geometry, select methods or determine tolerances. D39–D50 distinguish development/calibration seeds from reserved 17901–17905. The PI closure leaves these reserved seeds unused; a later project must explicitly register its own schedule.
7. **Independently audit each completed batch before interpretation.** Reconstruct from saved arrays and checkpoints, verify source/input/output hashes, weights, transforms and analytical answers where available. Do not merely reread the producer's summaries. D2–D6 establish the P16 reconstruction; D33/D34, D38, D46 and D48–D50 provide later audits. Hash discrepancies must be resolved, not silently refreshed.
8. **Enforce runtime boundaries.** Use tested SIGALRM or an equivalent process deadline, bounded batches and checkpoints, and only one expensive job at a time. D15's 3.6-hour rejection benchmark exceeded its intended budget: this was a workflow failure. D16 introduced the deadline test; later conditional resumptions had recorded finite caps. D43's test-result ordering failure is also retained. A failed or timed-out job is not permission for an unlimited restart.
9. **Keep a diagnostic's scientific target unchanged.** No alteration of likelihood, prior, biological parameter, medium, allocation or maintenance to help it pass. D12 bars an infeasibility penalty within P17; D26 marks nuisance refresh as a diagnostic, not repair. The target-revision specification is a separate proposal requiring PI approval.
10. **Record the precise blocker instead of manufacturing a decisive answer.** D38 shows short real chains do not certify prior recovery; D44/D45 block direct transfer of the tested quadratic fit; the PI closure records that full correction confirmation remains unmet. Do not convert cessation into validation.

D44/D45 concern reproducible scale-dependent curvature at **one non-optimal point**, not proof that all samplers fail or that likelihood structure is the sole cause. The **80–87% living fractions are analytical toy outcomes**, never a revised biological target or acceptance threshold. P16/P17 condition on **dTm=0**, assuming an exact meltome mean; uniform melting-temperature uncertainty is excluded and would otherwise be absorbed by tm_scale and catalytic parameters.
